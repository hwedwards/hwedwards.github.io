The task

The agents are instructed to build a five-block-wide, three-block-high oak wall with a central doorway. Each worker receives its own camera view, inventory, memory and messages, then chooses a short program from a bounded skill vocabulary.

The trial tests more than initial block placement. Workers share supplies through a chest. After a controlled intervention removes three blocks, they arrange another material handoff and restore the wall.

Engineering focus

I built a small, auditable coordination experiment around TeamCraft. The focus is the interface between model decisions, shared work ownership, action execution and evidence of what actually happened.

  • Bounded actions. Candidate programs are validated before they are compiled into simulator skill calls. Models cannot execute arbitrary generated code.
  • Explicit coordination. Workers claim construction sections and acknowledge the current ownership table before building.
  • Observable execution. Receipts record actions, failures, camera freshness and material transfers.
  • Separate evaluation. The evaluator checks the structure independently; its voxel state and success flags are not supplied to the policies.

What was verified

Two selected fresh-world trials completed the same stronger supply-and-repair condition with unchanged source. Each used 13 rounds and 26 model calls. The checks verified the wall, initial handoff, post-damage resupply and recovery.

The publication audit regenerated the evidence summaries, matched the current source to the recorded run hashes, passed 86 Python tests and all four JavaScript test files, and fully decoded the derived demonstration video.

These are two selected successful repetitions of one fixed-site condition. They are not an overall success rate or a general construction benchmark; earlier development failures are outside that selected pair.

Scope & limits

This is a simulation on a fixed, calibrated worksite. It uses simulator pathfinding and placement, centralized coordination and serialized action effects. Workers receive inventory and execution information as well as images, so the system is not pixels-only.

The demonstration does not establish physical construction autonomy, generalization to new layouts, distributed consensus, dynamic task reassignment or persistent skill learning. It is a bounded experiment in coordinating agents and making their progress and failure auditable.

The result

A traceable demonstration of two visual agents coordinating construction, sharing materials and recovering from a controlled disturbance.
